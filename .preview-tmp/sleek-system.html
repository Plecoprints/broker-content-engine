  1. 01

    Insuring a 60-Foot Catamaran in the Med: What Underwriters Actually Ask

    Multihull cover is priced on beam, berth agreement and named-skipper qualification far more than on length, which is why a quote for a 60-foot catamaran and a 60-foot monohull rarely resemble each other. The piece walks through what a Mediterranean underwriter reviews line by line — hull configuration, winter berth, crew endorsement, cruising limits — and which of those an owner can still change before renewal.

    Why your readers would want it

    Buyers raise insurance late, usually once an offer is already in and the survey is booked. Being the person who explained the underwriting logic first reads as advice rather than sales, and it removes one of the commonest reasons a Mediterranean deal stalls through September.

  2. 02

    Monohull or Multihull: The Six Questions That Decide It

    Almost every comparison online argues for one hull form. This one refuses to. It sets out the six questions whose answers make the decision for the buyer — intended cruising ground, guest count, berthing budget, tolerance for sea state, resale window, and whether there will be permanent crew — and states the trade-off honestly in both directions, including where a monohull is simply the better boat.

    Why your readers would want it

    It is the comparison your clients have already half-read somewhere worse. Sending a version that declines to pick a side positions you as the broker who asks better questions than the listing portals, and it is the single most forwarded piece in this category.

    Searches sailing monohull vs. catamaran monohull catamaran hull characteristics
  3. 03

    Reading a Mediterranean Charter Programme Before You Commit to One

    More owners now buy with a charter programme in mind, then discover the programme quietly dictates layout, crew berths and which weeks of August they can use themselves. This reads a typical Croatia-and-Ionian season from the owner's side of the table — realistic occupancy, MYBA terms, what a central agent needs the boat to have — and separates the numbers that hold from the ones that flatter.

    Why your readers would want it

    Charter economics is where owners feel most exposed to optimism, and the client who trusts your arithmetic on a programme brings you the purchase. It also earns links from the charter-side operators you already work with in Split and Athens.

    Searches catamaran charter mediterranean catamaran yacht charter croatia crewed catamaran charter
  4. 04

    Financing a Multihull: Why the Structure Matters More Than the Rate

    Marine lenders treat multihulls differently from monohulls — deposit expectations, flag and ownership structure, and whether the boat will charter move the final terms more than the headline rate does. The article lays out the three structures Mediterranean buyers actually use, what each costs in flexibility, and the questions to put to a lender before an offer rather than after.

    Why your readers would want it

    Finance conversations usually happen away from the broker, with an adviser who does not know boats. Being the one who framed the structure keeps you in the room at exactly the point where these deals are won or lost.
