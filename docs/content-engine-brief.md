# Brief: Broker Partner Content Engine

Context for producing a leadership-facing graphic. Plain text, copy/paste ready.

## Audience and purpose

Sunreef Yachts leadership. Explain what we are building, how it works, and what it does
for the brand. Confident and concrete, not promotional. Smart readers, no technical
background, sixty seconds of attention.

## The one-sentence version

An engine that studies how each of our broker and dealer partners already writes, then
hands them finished, publication-ready articles about catamarans — featuring Sunreef, in
their own voice, built to be found in search.

## The problem

Sunreef sells through a network of broker and dealer partners. Each has their own website,
blog and email list — audiences of qualified, in-market buyers we do not reach directly.

We have never given those partners anything consistent to publish. A broker who wants to
write about Sunreef has to research it, write it and optimise it themselves. Most
therefore write nothing, or write something thin.

The result: the people closest to our buyers are the quietest voices in our category.

## The central insight

A buyer researching a catamaran reads brokers, not manufacturers. When our story appears
on a partner's site it carries *their* credibility — worth considerably more than the same
words on sunreef-yachts.com.

## How it works — five stages, weekly cycle

1. **Qualify** — identify brokers selling at our level (60ft+) who already run a blog or newsletter.
2. **Listen** — read their published writing; learn register, sentence rhythm, typical length, structure, vocabulary, themes.
3. **Target** — select topics their buyers search for *and* their site can realistically rank for.
4. **Write** — three publication-ready pieces in their voice, featuring Sunreef naturally.
5. **Check** — eight automated gates, including one that refuses any specific claim about a named Sunreef vessel. A Sunreef marketer reads the output — all of it through the first pilot — and a human sends every piece. Nothing is sent automatically.

## What each partner receives — three formats

| Format | Destination | Length | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pillar | Their blog, cornerstone piece | 2,000–2,300 words | Depth and long-term search visibility |
| Feature | Their blog, regular post | Matched to their typical length | The one that must genuinely pass as theirs |
| Newsletter | Their email list | 100–200 words | For many partners, the channel they use weekly |

All three derive from the same source article, so the message never diverges.

## Quality control — seven automated checks

Keyword selection:
1. Search difficulty below 30 — the broker can realistically rank
2. Search volume above 100/month — real demand exists
3. On-category — excludes terms scoring well numerically but not about 60ft+ luxury catamarans
4. Editorial intent — excludes purely transactional terms; brokers publish articles, not adverts

Content originality:
5. Unique — against every article the system has produced, so no two partners get the same piece
6. Tailored — measured against that broker's own writing style
7. Original — against the broker's own published work, so we never return a version of their own writing

Then a human approves. Every time.

## Market data

Semrush, US database, September 2026. 243 terms analysed, 148 eligible after filtering,
34,920 combined monthly searches.

**The strategic finding: the broad luxury-yacht category is closed to us. Catamarans are wide open.**

Open — winnable, difficulty under 30:

| Term | Monthly | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| catamaran for sale | 8,100 | 25 |
| catamarans for sale | 4,400 | 24 |
| power catamaran for sale | 2,400 | 17 |
| difference between a yacht and a sailboat | 2,400 | 6 |
| what is a catamaran | 1,900 | 25 |
| yacht refit | 1,900 | 10 |
| catamaran insurance | 390 | 7 |
| how to finance a catamaran | 260 | 3 |

Closed — unreachable, difficulty 30+:

| Term | Monthly | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| luxury yacht charter | 8,100 | 60 |
| yacht broker | 5,400 | 78 |
| boat show | 5,400 | 76 |
| sailing catamaran | 3,600 | 41 |
| yacht financing | 1,000 | 37 |

Note the pairing: `catamaran for sale` and `luxury yacht charter` have identical volume
(8,100) and opposite verdicts (difficulty 25 vs 60). Same demand, one reachable and one
not. This contrast is the single most useful thing to visualise.

Demand sitting directly on our differentiator — approx. 1,460 searches/month, low
competition, currently unclaimed:

| Term | Monthly | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| solar catamaran | 480 | 23 |
| solar powered catamaran | 480 | 21 |
| electric catamaran | 390 | 20 |
| hybrid catamaran | 110 | 13 |

This points squarely at Sunreef Eco — not a generic catamaran claim any builder could make.

## What it does for the brand

- **Presence where our buyers already look** — third-party sites carrying third-party trust
- **Reach into audiences we do not own** — partner blogs and email lists
- **Consistency** — every partner, every cycle, replacing sporadic self-serve effort
- **Stronger partnerships** — we become the manufacturer that helps them sell
- **Search ground we can hold** — visibility on the terms our category is built on

## Closing line

From a manufacturer that sells *through* brokers, to one that markets *through* them.

## Status

Built and tested. Next step is a live pilot with real partners. A broker-facing portal,
where partners log in and collect their content, is committed and not yet built.

## Suggested visual structure

1. Headline plus the one-sentence version
2. The five-stage process as a connected horizontal flow
3. Hero diagram: one Sunreef story → three formats → fanning out to many partner sites → each reaching its own audience
4. The open-vs-closed search comparison as paired bars (identical volume, opposite outcome)
5. Four value figures: 34,920 / 3 formats / 1,460 / 8 checks
6. Closing strategic shift line

## Things to avoid

Do not lead with the technology — lead with the underused partner network. Do not mention
costs; they are not the argument. Do not imply content publishes automatically; a human sends
every piece, and that matters to anyone worried about brand risk.

Be precise about the control model, because an earlier version of this brief was not. There is
no per-piece sign-off: a draft is cleared by eight automated gates — one of which refuses any
specific claim about a named Sunreef vessel, so a fabricated figure cannot reach a broker — and a
marketer reads the output rather than approving each item, at 100% of it through the first pilot.
Two further judge gates are designed and not built. Say that. It is a stronger story than a
sign-off promise, and unlike a sign-off promise it is true.
