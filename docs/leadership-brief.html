The gap

Brokers sell our boats. They are not equipped to market them.

A broker who wants to write about Sunreef has to research it, write it and optimise it themselves — so most write nothing, or write something thin. The result is that the people closest to our buyers are the quietest voices in our category. This engine removes the work: it studies how each broker already writes, then produces publication-ready articles in their own voice, about catamarans, featuring Sunreef.

The opportunity

The luxury yacht category is closed. Catamarans are wide open.

We analysed 243 search terms against live market data. The pattern is decisive: the broad luxury-yacht terms are dominated by publishers and portals our brokers cannot outrank. The catamaran terms — our actual category — are winnable today.

A finding worth acting on

The strongest open ground sits directly on our differentiator.

Solar, electric and hybrid catamaran searches total roughly 1,460 a month at difficulty 13–23 — low-competition demand pointing squarely at Sunreef Eco. That is not a generic catamaran claim any builder could make. It is our category, and nobody has taken it.

What each partner receives

One story, three ways to publish it.

Brokers differ in what they can actually use. A large brokerage runs a blog; a smaller one has only a newsletter. Every partner gets all three, drawn from the same article so the message never diverges.

Pillar

2,000–2,300 words

A cornerstone article for their blog, built for depth and search visibility. Written to rank, and to stay relevant for years.

Feature

Matched to their usual length

A regular post written to read like they wrote it — matched to their register, sentence rhythm and article structure. This is the one that has to pass as theirs.

Newsletter

100–200 words

A compressed version for their email list. For many partners this is the channel they actually use every week.

Brand protection

Nothing reaches a partner that has not survived eight checks.

Content published under a broker’s name still reflects on ours. Every draft must clear eight automated checks before it can be delivered — including one that refuses any specific claim about a named Sunreef vessel, so a fabricated length or certification cannot reach a partner’s blog. A Sunreef marketer reads the output, at 100% of it through the first pilot, and a human sends every piece: nothing is delivered automatically.

07

Original

Compared against the broker’s own published work, so we never hand them back a version of something they already wrote.

08

No product claims

Refuses any specific figure or certification attached to a named Sunreef vessel — no length, no capacity, no class notation. Our articles make the case for the category, not claims about a boat that a reader could act on and we could get wrong.

Two further checks — an editorial-value judge and a brand-quality judge — are designed and not yet built. Until they are, a marketer reads every piece the system produces.

Economics

The cost is not a meaningful line item.

At a cadence of 30 brokers a week — a pillar article, a feature and a newsletter for each — the entire production cost is below what the team spends on coffee.

What this buys us

Brand presence in the places our buyers already look.

Every article a partner publishes puts Sunreef in front of an audience that trusts them — which is worth considerably more than the same words on our own site. It gives brokers a reason to talk about us to their list, week after week, and it puts our name against the search terms our category is actually built on.

The engine is built and tested. The remaining step is a live pilot against real partners.
